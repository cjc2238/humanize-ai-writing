# Surface Tells

The lexical and syntactic layer. These are real, but they are the *easy* tells —
modern models increasingly avoid them and a find-and-replace defeats them, which
is exactly why they're insufficient on their own. Clean them *after* the
structural edits in SKILL.md, not instead of them.

## Overused words and phrases
Treat as yellow flags, not bans — one is fine, a cluster is a tell.

- delve, delving
- tapestry, rich tapestry
- testament (a testament to)
- realm, landscape ("in the realm of," "the ever-evolving landscape of")
- navigate, navigating (the complexities of)
- underscore, underscores
- pivotal, crucial, vital, essential, key
- vibrant, bustling
- meticulous, meticulously
- nestled
- boasts, boasting
- seamless, seamlessly
- robust, leverage, utilize (prefer: use)
- foster, fostering
- intricate, multifaceted
- resonate, resonates
- beacon, cornerstone, treasure trove

## Sentence constructions

- **"Not just X, but Y"** and **"It's not about X, it's about Y."** The single
  most recognizable current tell. Rewrite flat.
- **Triadic lists** — always exactly three parallel items ("fast, reliable, and
  scalable"). Vary the count; sometimes two, sometimes four, sometimes one.
- **The setup-question-then-tidy-answer** — "But what does this mean? It means…"
- **Antithesis balance** — every sentence weighing two neat halves.
- **Participial tails** — "…, highlighting the importance of…", "…, making it a
  valuable…", "…, ensuring that…". AI loves to append a gloss this way.

## Transitions and connective tissue

- Uniform formal transitions: Moreover, Furthermore, Additionally, However,
  In conclusion, Ultimately. Humans use fewer and more varied connectors, and
  often none.
- Signposting overload: "First… Second… Finally…" when the ideas don't need it.

## Openers and closers to cut

- "In today's world / In the modern era / In an age of…"
- "It's important to note that / It's worth mentioning that…"
- "Whether you're a X or a Y…"
- Closing: "In conclusion," "Ultimately," "At the end of the day," and any final
  paragraph that restates the piece or adds an inspirational bow ("a reminder
  that…", "one thing is clear…").

## Punctuation and formatting

- Em-dash overuse (—) for emphasis in every other sentence.
- Perfectly even paragraph lengths.
- Reflexive bolding of key terms; bullet lists where prose would do.
- Emoji section headers in places a person wouldn't use them.
