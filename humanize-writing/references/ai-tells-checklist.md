# AI Tells Checklist

A diagnostic pass. Read the draft and check each item. Higher-severity tells do
more to mark text as machine-written, so fix those first. Use the same list as a
pre-flight when generating.

## Tier 1 — Structural (hardest to detect, most durable; fix first)

- [ ] **Over-explained point.** Is there a sentence that states the moral,
      lesson, or thesis outright — especially a closing one that restates the
      opening? AI does this ~77% of the time vs. ~52% for humans. *Fix: cut it;
      let the reader infer.*
- [ ] **Too linear.** Does everything proceed cause → effect → neat conclusion
      with no digression, subplot, or time jump? 79% of AI texts have no
      subplots vs. 57% for humans. *Fix: add a tangent, start mid-stream, leave
      a thread open.*
- [ ] **Everything resolves.** Is every tension tied off, every question
      answered, the ending reconciled? *Fix: leave one thing unresolved or
      morally mixed.*
- [ ] **No reader in the room.** Does the text never address or acknowledge the
      reader? Humans do so ~28% vs. 7% for AI. *Fix: one genuine aside or
      first-person intrusion.*
- [ ] **Morally tidy.** Is everyone reasonable and the takeaway uplifting? *Fix:
      allow ambivalence; let someone be partly wrong.*

## Tier 2 — Texture (strong signals, moderately visible)

- [ ] **Sensory over-rendering.** Emotion shown only through body and
      environment (tight chest, cold palms, thick air, dimming light)? AI uses
      bodily metaphor 81% vs. 38%; explicit emotion words only 8% vs. 29% for
      humans. *Fix: name some feelings flatly; cut half the sensory inventory.*
- [ ] **Vague world.** Generic stand-ins ("a popular app," "a major city," "a
      classic novel") instead of real, specific references? Humans name the real
      thing ~47% vs. 24%. *Fix: use actual names, dates, places, prices.*
- [ ] **Uniform rhythm.** Even sentence lengths, even paragraph sizes, measured
      tone throughout? *Fix: make it lumpy — long then short, a fragment, an
      uneven section.*

## Tier 3 — Surface (easy to spot and fix; necessary but not sufficient)

- [ ] Overused vocabulary (delve, tapestry, testament, realm, navigate,
      landscape, underscore, pivotal, vibrant, meticulous, nestled, boasts).
- [ ] "Not just X, but Y" / "it's not about X, it's about Y."
- [ ] Everything in threes (triadic parallel lists).
- [ ] Em-dash overuse and uniform transition words (Moreover, Furthermore,
      In conclusion).
- [ ] Throat-clearing openers ("It's important to note," "In today's world,"
      "In the ever-evolving landscape of").
- [ ] A closing paragraph that summarizes what was just said.

See `surface-tells.md` for the full surface list and `the-research.md` for where
these numbers come from.
